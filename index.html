How Effective Is the MLB ABS (Automated Ball-Strike) System?

A data analysis project evaluating the accuracy and impact of the automated ball-strike system in minor league baseball, using public data from MLB and Baseball Savant.

View the GitHub repository Jump to findings

Overview & Objective

MLB's Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) Challenge System lets a batter, pitcher or catcher challenge a ball or strike call, and Hawk-Eye tracking settles it. Each team gets a limited number of challenges, and a team keeps a challenge when it wins. The system was tested for years in the minor leagues before reaching the majors in 2026. This project looks at how it performed in Triple-A, where a full season of challenge data is public, and compares it with spring training and the 2026 MLB season.

  • How often are challenges made, and how often do they overturn the umpire?
  • Do batters and fielders (mostly catchers) challenge equally well?
  • Does game situation, such as the count or the inning, change who wins a challenge?

Data Collection & Cleaning

The data comes from MLB's public Baseball Savant ABS challenge leaderboard, which reports challenges and overturned calls by level, season, player and team. The count, inning and height breakdowns in the charts below come from MLB.com's published analysis of the same Triple-A season.

  • Collect

    I downloaded the Triple-A 2025 batter table from Baseball Savant as a CSV file (it covers challenges made by batters and challenges made against them) and loaded it in Python on Google Colab.

  • Clean

    I used pandas to verify the file, checked every overturn rate against the raw counts, reshaped it to one row per batter and role, and dropped rows with no challenges.

  • Query

    I loaded the cleaned table into SQLite and used SQL to total challenges and overturns by role and team, then compared the results with MLB.com's published figures.

Key Findings & Impact

  • Challenges are rare. MLB.com counted 9,432 challenges on about 861,000 Triple-A pitches, so roughly 1% of pitches were challenged and 0.5% were overturned.
  • Umpires are hard to beat. Players pick only the pitches they think were missed, yet the overturn rate was only 49.4% in the Baseball Savant data. MLB.com also found a challenge in 98.5% of games, but two or fewer overturns in 61% of games.
  • Fielders challenge better than batters. Fielders won 53.7% of challenges and batters 45.1%. The same gap showed up in 2026 spring training (60% vs 45%), and AP reported a 54% overturn rate in MLB's first regular season with catchers more successful than batters.
  • Desperation hurts. Batters won 51% of challenges in most counts but only 36% on two strikes and 34% on full counts.
  • Skill varies widely. Among Triple-A teams, batting challenge success ranged from 36.6% (Louisville Bats) to 55.0% (Oklahoma City Comets).

Impact

Although few pitches were challenged, the ones that were mattered. Challenges reversed 1,239 strikeouts and 584 walks in Triple-A in 2025, and 323 full-count pitches flipped directly between strikeout and walk. The system corrects the clearest misses while leaving most of the umpire's job, and receiving skill, in place.

Limitations

An overturn rate is not an umpire accuracy rate, because players choose which pitches to challenge. Minor league players and umpires also differ from major leaguers, so Triple-A results may not carry over exactly.
